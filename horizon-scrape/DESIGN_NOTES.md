# Horizon-Scrape — Design Notes

## Product promise
Make the air’s reduced visibility visible—while showing exactly what a photo can and cannot tell you.

## Design priorities
1. Make the primary task obvious within the first viewport.
2. Make the central visualization interactive and meaningful.
3. Keep explanations adjacent to the data or decision they explain.
4. Distinguish live, stale, simulated, and unavailable data.
5. Provide a useful non-3D/non-map path for critical information.
6. Treat mobile, keyboard navigation, and reduced motion as first-class design constraints.

## Accent token
`#FFD600`

## Before high-fidelity UI
- Sketch the primary journey in 5–7 frames.
- Design desktop and mobile composition separately.
- Define typography, spacing, surface, border, focus, and status tokens.
- Create realistic loading, empty, error, and demo-data states.
- Test the main journey with someone who has not read the project brief.

## Visual north star
An editorial environmental observatory: warm yellow as a high-visibility accent, dark ink or slate surfaces, horizon photography as the emotional anchor, and restrained chart/map overlays. Use a large image comparison stage—original versus processed—alongside a clean time-series view. Avoid making a dramatic heat map look more scientifically certain than the underlying observations support.
